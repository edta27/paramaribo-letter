How Low Can Bitcoin Go? 2026 Crash and Bottom Scenarios

Viral posts call for $38k or $28k Bitcoin. The letter puts each crash call on trial: what the tape shows, what it would take, and how much weight it deserves.

11 letters · updated September 26, 2026

Every Bitcoin drawdown brings a wave of crash targets: $46k, $38k, $28k. Some come from shrinking-drawdown extrapolations, some from macro worry about expensive equities, high yields and leverage. The letter's job is to separate a crash mechanism from a crash probability.

The desk's rule is that vulnerability is not probability. Elevated valuations, rising yields and rebuilt leverage can make a market fragile, but none of them is evidence that a cascade is likely on its own. A crash call has to be earned in sequence: each support level fails on a daily close, liquidations rise in intensity, ETF demand turns into sustained redemptions, and the 2026 low near $57,700 breaks without a reclaim.

Through September 2026 the letter's base case has been correction, not collapse. A 10% to 20% pullback stays live; a new cycle low is weighted as a tail of roughly 15%; extreme targets like $28k to $38k stay severe-tail references until every door above them fails. The issues below show how those weights were set and changed as each new data point arrived, including where the desk was wrong.

If you want the short version, read the pinned issues first. They lay out the scenario weights, the falsification tests and the case for and against $58k having been the bottom.
