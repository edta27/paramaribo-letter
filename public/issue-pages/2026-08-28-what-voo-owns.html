Desk note: VOO already owns the Nvidia day

Vanguard’s S&P 500 ETF semi-annual through June 30 shows $1.68T and 38% information technology. That is concentration already on the books — not a Warsh catalyst.

August 28, 2026 · The Paramaribo Letter

Short equity-desk note for Friday 28 August, before Warsh. Source: Vanguard 500 Index Fund ETF Shares (VOO) semi-annual shareholder report for January 1 to June 30, 2026, delivered through a Say Technologies regulated-documents link. This is fund literature, not a vote ballot and not a Bitcoin print. Cash remains valid. Full morning letter: Issue 07.

What the report says

As of June 30, VOO’s ETF share class reported about $1.675 trillion in fund net assets, 519 holdings, and 1% portfolio turnover. Cost on a hypothetical $10,000 over six months was $2, or 0.03% annualized. Sector mix was still an information-technology tape: IT 38.0%, Financials 11.6%, Communication Services 9.7%, Consumer Discretionary 9.3%, Health Care and Industrials 8.9% each. Energy was 3.0%. The one structural change named: effective January 12, 2026, Vanguard runs portfolio management through its wholly-owned subsidiary Vanguard Capital Management, LLC — an entity line, not a strategy rewrite.

Thirty-eight percent IT in the default S&P sleeve means Thursday’s Nvidia session was already inside the institutional index book.

What it does not say

No holdings list in this summary sheet. No Nvidia weight. No forward guidance. No rates view. No crypto sleeve. It does not move the desk’s Bitcoin box ($70,000–$82,000) and it does not tell you how Warsh will print at 10:00 a.m. Eastern.
