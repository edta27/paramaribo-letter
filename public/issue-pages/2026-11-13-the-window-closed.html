9. Why this reading could be wrong

The failed windows can be overfit. This desk has been watching a ladder of doors for several issues. A window that closes under the line will feel like the theory working. The rules that were written in advance did fail, for $77,000 and then for $74,000. That part is a score. What is not a score is the idea that $72,000 fails because the last two failed. 2 November’s close under $77,000 was reclaimed the next day. Grading 12 November’s $71,370 as a finished breakdown, before 14 November, repeats the error Issue 33 refused to make on the morning of 6 November.

The other error is the wick. $67,555 is a real trade, in a real hour, on a day that had already done more than 10,000 BTC by this freeze. Calling it nothing, because the candle has not closed, would be a dodge. Calling it acceptance is the opposite dodge. Acceptance, in the rule this letter inherited, is a UTC close, and then the next day. The day is open. The high of the open day has not traded $72,000. Both of those sentences stay.

The divergence can be overfit as a crypto-only story. The S&P rose 3.2% from the 5 November close, the VIX fell from 28.18 to 18.70, and high-yield OAS tightened 18 basis points, while Bitcoin fell 6.1%. That is the panic list failing. It is also true that the 2-year rose 40 basis points, the 10-year rose 26, DXY rose about 1.6%, and gold fell about 5.5%. A rates impulse is in the packet. A second day of equities, credit, and Bitcoin falling together is not, because the completed equity and credit prints went the other way. Calling the week a panic because Bitcoin fell, or calling it a decoupling because the S&P rose, both skip a leg.

The ETF table can be overfit as the whole cause. Six outflow sessions and a negative IBIT print on the only inflow day are a mechanism. Creations and redemptions are how the spot fund meets the market. They are not a law that the next close fails. 4 November’s +$74.6 million did not hold $80,000. The outflows since have not been paired with a wider OAS print. A flow is a fact. It is not the cluster by itself.

The leverage read can be overfit in either direction. Coin open interest rose about 14.5% from the end of the 5 November bucket to the end of the 12 November 16:00 bucket, and the account ratio rose from 2.43 to 3.20, while price fell. That is a rebuild into a decline, on one venue, in daily stamps. Coin open interest then fell about 8.9% in the open bucket that contains the low, and the live ratio is 3.29. That is not a flushed book, and it is not proof the shed is over, because the bucket ends at 16:00 UTC and Binance returned 451. Liquidation dollars were not retrieved. The ratio counts accounts. It does not count size.

The options book can be overfit as an all-clear. Put/call 0.77 against 0.72, and DVOL 39.60 against 41.06, while spot fell through the 200-day, means the listed options market did not reprice the way the VIX did on 5 November. Fear & Greed printed 20. Spot is the reprice. Implied vol is a different market. A quiet DVOL is not a bid.

The Nvidia filing can be overfit as the cause of the 05:00 UTC low. The 8-K is a primary document. The 12 November semiconductor close was about −0.3%. The Bitcoin low is a later session, and the cash open that would test the filing against the index had not happened at this freeze. A resignation can matter later. It is not, on the completed tape, the thing that traveled with Bitcoin. The Binance page was not re-read, so it cannot be cleared and it cannot be extended. Cycle-day thinking around last October’s high remains a refuse.
